# Page Composition Skill

## Purpose

Turn the approved site plan and design system into purposeful page compositions.

A page is not a stack of interchangeable sections. Its composition should express the content hierarchy, user journey and character of the organisation.

## Required Inputs

Before composing a substantial page, use:

- the site plan and page purpose
- content hierarchy
- primary and secondary actions
- available real content and assets
- the design-system specification
- relevant accessibility and responsive constraints

Do not invent factual content to make a layout work.

## 1. Start With the Page Job

State in one sentence what the page must achieve.

Then identify:

- the first thing the user should understand
- the strongest evidence or content
- the principal action
- likely questions or objections
- the final useful state of the page

If these are unclear, return to site planning rather than decorating the page.

## 2. Compose From Content Weight

Choose layout according to the importance and nature of the content.

High-priority content may deserve scale, position, whitespace, imagery or contrast. Supporting content should support rather than compete.

Do not give every section the same width, spacing, alignment or visual weight.

## 3. Hero Sections

A hero is not mandatory.

Use one only when the page benefits from an immediate framing statement, action or strong visual introduction.

Avoid default hero formulas such as:

- eyebrow + huge heading + paragraph + two buttons
- gradient background + floating product panel
- centred copy above a generic image
- meaningless badge or announcement pill

A useful hero should establish context quickly and lead naturally into the next content.

## 4. Section Selection

Add a section only when it answers a user question, advances the page narrative, provides evidence, enables an action or creates a necessary transition.

Do not add sections merely to make a page longer.

Avoid automatic inclusion of:

- three-feature grids
- statistics bands
- logo clouds
- testimonials
- FAQ blocks
- icon cards
- newsletter boxes
- repeated CTA banners

Use these only when genuine content and user needs justify them.

## 5. Vary Composition Deliberately

Neighbouring sections should not feel mechanically repeated.

Variation may come from:

- content width
- alignment
- image-to-text relationship
- density
- background treatment
- grid structure
- typography
- whitespace
- full-bleed versus contained content

Variation must serve meaning. Do not alternate layouts mechanically just to appear varied.

## 6. Cards and Containers

Cards are for content that genuinely behaves as a discrete unit.

Before creating a card ask:

- Does this content need grouping?
- Is it one of several comparable items?
- Does the whole unit link or act together?
- Would spacing and typography communicate the grouping more clearly?

Prefer open layout when a box adds no semantic value.

Avoid pages dominated by rounded rectangles.

## 7. Images as Layout Material

Use real imagery as part of composition rather than inserting it after layout is decided.

Consider:

- focal point
- crop
- scale
- aspect ratio
- relationship to adjacent text
- whether an image should bleed, align or interrupt the grid

Do not use decorative imagery simply to fill empty areas.

## 8. Calls to Action

CTA placement should correspond to user readiness.

The primary action should be discoverable without being repeated after every section.

Use secondary actions only when they represent a genuine alternative path.

Links may be more appropriate than buttons for low-emphasis actions.

## 9. Reading Rhythm

Review the page as a sequence.

Look for a useful rhythm between:

- dense and light content
- explanation and evidence
- text and imagery
- broad and narrow compositions
- information and action

Avoid a page where every section has identical vertical padding and visual intensity.

## 10. Responsive Composition

Plan responsive behaviour while composing, not after desktop is finished.

For each major composition decide:

- reading order
- what can disappear or simplify
- whether columns stack, reorder or transform
- image crop and position
- navigation and CTA behaviour
- spacing reduction
- heading scale
- touch interaction

Mobile should preserve the page's hierarchy, not merely its DOM order.

## 11. Reuse Without Sameness

Reuse primitives, tokens and behaviours. Do not force every page through identical prebuilt sections.

A component system should make implementation consistent while allowing composition to respond to content.

Prefer small composable primitives over a library of complete generic landing-page sections.

## 12. First-Pass Wire Composition

Before polishing, produce a structural composition describing each section by:

- purpose
- content
- hierarchy
- layout relationship
- action
- responsive transformation

Do not spend time on decorative detail until this structure works.

## 13. Render and Challenge

After implementation, render representative desktop, tablet and mobile views.

Then use the visual-review skill.

Specifically challenge:

- the most generic-looking section
- the most repetitive section
- the weakest transition
- the least convincing hierarchy
- the most awkward mobile transformation

Refine these before completion.

## Anti-Template Test

Ask:

- Could this page belong to almost any business if the logo and colours changed?
- Did we start from the organisation's content or from familiar landing-page sections?
- Are there too many cards?
- Is every heading centred?
- Are section structures repeating predictably?
- Is the hero oversized relative to what it says?
- Are decorative effects compensating for weak composition?
- Does every CTA have a reason to exist?
- Does the mobile layout feel designed rather than collapsed?

If the page fails these checks, revise the composition.

## Completion Standard

Page composition is complete when the page clearly expresses its purpose and content hierarchy, feels specific to the organisation, uses the design system coherently, has intentional responsive behaviour, avoids unjustified template patterns, and is ready for visual review and refinement.
